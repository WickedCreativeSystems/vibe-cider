Which letters the words practise

Between them, the twenty words use 21 of the 26 lowercase letters. The five they leave out are j, q, v, x and z. To practise every letter, add jacket, quilt, van, box and zeppelin to the words in the book maker: each has a picture, and each brings in one of the missing letters.

Tall letters and tails

On the four-line guides, seven lowercase letters rise to the headline: b, d, f, h, k, l and t. Five drop below the baseline: g, j, p, q and y. The other fourteen sit between the baseline and the dashed midline, apart from the dot on an i. One of these words stays between the baseline and the midline all the way: snowman. Every letter in it is the same height. Eighteen of these words reach up to the headline: ball (b and l), star (t), gift (f and t), bell (b and l), sock (k), home (h), book (b and k), candy (d), train (t), robot (b and t), cookie (k), candle (d and l), sleigh (l and h), reindeer (d), ornament (t), lollipop (l), snowflake (f, l and k) and gingerbread (b and d). Six of these words drop below the baseline: mug (g), gift (g), candy (y), sleigh (g), lollipop (p) and gingerbread (g).

The words by length and strokes

A stroke is one movement of the pencil, from putting it down to lifting it, and each letter on the page shows its strokes with numbered start dots. An i or j takes two: the line, then the dot. These words run from 3 to 12 strokes, 134 in all.

Ordered by strokes instead, fewest first, the words go mug, ball, bell, home, star, sock, book, candy, robot, candle, gift, train, sleigh, snowman, cookie, reindeer, ornament, lollipop, snowflake and gingerbread. Words with the same count keep their order above. The biggest change is gift: it has four letters but takes 7 strokes, as i, f and t take two each, so it moves from fourth to eleventh. Selling a Christmas tracing book on KDP

Trace Press makes the whole book: A to Z, then these words a page each, with a matching full-wrap cover, in any of six KDP trim sizes. The button below opens it with the twenty words already typed in, for a 46-page book. Change the words, add numbers 0 to 9 or a “This book belongs to” page, and download. For the cover's size at that page count, see tracing book cover size. There are Halloween tracing worksheets too, made the same way, and Thanksgiving tracing worksheets.

A word gets a picture when Trace Press has one for it: 168 everyday words so far, such as dog, train and umbrella. All twenty pictures on this page are from Tabler Icons, under the MIT licence. Four words use a picture with another name: ball is a basketball, reindeer is a deer's head, ornament is a Christmas bauble and gingerbread is a gingerbread man. Words you add get theirs from the same two sets, except quilt, which Trace Press drew. Both allow copies to be sold. The licence texts: Tabler Icons, Lucide.

To be on sale by 25 December, count KDP's own times. Its Timelines help page says "it takes 3 business days for your book (or update) to be live on Amazon", and that publishing "may take up to 6 days" for an account that has never published a book. It also says low-content book reviews are taking longer than usual, three to four weeks. KDP's low-content definition is books "designed to be filled in by the user", and it doesn't name tracing books either way, so you decide with the low-content checkbox. KDP also takes only 2 new paperbacks a week from each account for publication, counted when first submitted and reset every Sunday at 12:00 AM UTC (Create a Book help), so a seasonal book takes one of that week’s two. Check the Timelines page on the day you publish.

With the low-content box ticked, count four weeks of review back from the day you need the book. A reviewed book submitted after 30 October may not be on sale for Black Friday, 27 November; for 25 December itself, the same count puts the last safe day at about Friday 27 November. If you don’t tick it, KDP’s 3 business days (6 for a first book) put the last safe day for Black Friday at about Thursday 19 November.
