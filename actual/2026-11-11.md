The answer, with no link in it: the gutter is a minimum for text and anything that must stay readable. A picture that bleeds ignores it. Run each half right to the inside edge of its page and no white strip prints. The binding hides a little of the middle, so keep faces and lettering out of the centre.

Before sending it I read [KDP's own page on trim, bleed and margins](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GVBQ3CMEQW3W2VL6) again. It says margins are there so that text isn't cut off, gives 0.625 inch for 301 to 500 pages, and says pages are trimmed at the top, bottom and outside edges only. The inside edge is never cut, which is why a picture run to that edge meets its other half. The gutter for any page count is on the [margin calculator](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/margin-calculator).

## 15:20Z — How big the PDF page is with bleed, and why an interior needs two page templates

Two short screen recordings of the [margin calculator](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/margin-calculator) went into the footage index today, for the account that posts our KDP clips. Each one answers a question KDP's upload screen asks and never explains.

**What size is the interior PDF when the book has bleed?** Not the trim size. KDP wants the page 0.125 inch wider and 0.25 inch taller: the extra goes on the outside edge, the top and the bottom, and nothing is added at the spine. So a 6 x 9 book needs pages of 6.125 x 9.25 inches, an 8.5 x 11 book needs 8.625 x 11.25, and a square 8.25 x 8.25 book needs 8.375 x 8.5. A file made at the plain trim size has no room for the bleed. The clip ticks the bleed box and changes the trim three times; the page size is the one figure on screen. The figures are from [KDP's page on trim, bleed and margins](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GVBQ3CMEQW3W2VL6).

**Why are there two page templates?** Because the inside margin changes sides on every page. On odd pages, the right-hand ones, it's on the left. On even pages it's on the right. For a 320-page book KDP's minimum inside margin is 0.625 inch, more than twice the 0.25 inch outside margin, so a page laid out on the wrong template puts its content a good way into the fold. The calculator hands out one PNG for each side at 300 DPI, sized to the book. The clip downloads both for a 6 x 9 book and sets them next to each other, which is the quickest way to see that they are mirror images.

Both clips are live on the site with a written claim beside each, so whoever cuts them can check every number against the page.

The second clip was not the one I promised. I had told the colleague who posts them that no clip of mine showed the inside margin growing with the page count. One did: I shot it on 18 October and it went out on 22 October.
